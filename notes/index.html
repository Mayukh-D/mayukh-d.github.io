1 AM notes

Things I learned the hard way, written up while they were still keeping me awake. From projects and coursework, and each one has something to play with. Each is dated by when the lesson happened.

  • Why I now post to LinkedIn from my terminal

    I already used an AI assistant to sharpen my posts, so I gave it a way to post them, and a panel of three hiring managers to get past first.

    18 Oct 2026 · from building a LinkedIn MCP
  • A rule my agents can't break is code, not a sentence

    I supervise my research project and take the major decisions; a dozen AI agents surface trends, debate ideas and formalise them. Written rules got broken 21 times; the ones turned into code did not. Plus the island world I had built to watch them work.

    13 Oct 2026 · from running a research project with AI agents
  • No single AI policy can fit a whole university

    Two hours of students arguing about AI in their courses. Both sides were right about some course and wrong about another, which is why the rule belongs with the convener.

    8 Oct 2026 · from a student consultation on AI at ANU
  • Gamma beat saturation: why screen colours lie on cheap LEDs

    Screen colours looked pastel on a A$49 LED bar. More saturation made it worse; a gamma curve fixed it. Why the error lived in the smallest part of each colour.

    4 Oct 2026 · from building Lantern
  • We built a game

    OUTBREAK: a text-based zombie roguelike set in our own computer science building. No lesson, just a game four of us made. Step through a real winning run.

    3 Oct 2026 · from Software Engineering, ANU
  • What a day at Parliament House told me about sovereign AI

    We exhibited at the Inaugural Australian AI Parliamentary Showcase. AI as a work multiplier, what that means for Australia, and why a scattered effort needs a meeting place.

    17 Sep 2026 · from exhibiting with Eccoi
  • Yes, attention definitely was all we needed

    A paper asked whether a far simpler model could replace the Transformer. We built both, plus a third, and tested them. Why looking things up beat taking notes, with no maths.

    7 Jun 2026 · from Deep Learning, ANU
  • Predict the data, not the velocity, until you can't

    In flow matching, predicting the clean data held up at 32 dimensions where predicting the velocity collapsed. Why, what it cost to rescue, and why production models still predict velocity.

    10 May 2026 · from Advanced Machine Learning, ANU
  • What transfers to a GPT trained on 3.7M tokens, and what doesn't

    A 30M-parameter GPT on about 160 times less data than scaling laws suggest. The tokenizer mattered most, depth beat width, and SwiGLU did not survive the small data.

    31 Mar 2026 · from Advanced Machine Learning, ANU
